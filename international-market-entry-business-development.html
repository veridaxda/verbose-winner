Advisory practices/Market entry & development

International Market Entry & Business Development

Entering a new market means deciding where demand is credible, which route to market is practical and who can help execute it. We support energy and industrial businesses with market assessment, partner selection and commercial development, drawing on practical experience in the Gulf and West Africa.

Enquire about this practice →

When to engage us

Energy traders, industrial service firms, operators and investors exploring a new market

  • You are comparing markets and need to understand demand, competition, route costs and the barriers to doing business.
  • You need to choose between a direct approach, a distributor or a local commercial partnership.
  • You have identified a market and need to move from an initial opportunity list to qualified conversations and a credible pipeline.

What we do

Market assessment and prioritisation

Evaluate demand, buyers, competition, pricing and route economics. Identify regulatory and operating questions to take to local specialists.

Route to market and entry strategy

Compare direct sales, distribution and partnership options. Define target segments, entry requirements, milestones and what would justify further investment.

Partner and counterparty mapping

Build a shortlist against agreed criteria, assess commercial fit and prepare a plan for further diligence and introductions.

Business development support

Develop the commercial proposition, qualify opportunities and support outreach and discussions. Maintain a pipeline with clear next actions.

What you receive

We agree the outputs before work starts. Depending on the scope, they can include:

  • A market prioritisation and route-to-market assessment
  • A target-account list and partner shortlist
  • A phased entry plan with costs and milestones
  • A qualified opportunity pipeline with agreed next actions

Illustrative engagement

Choosing a route into a new market

An industrial services company considering West Africa needs to decide which buyer segments to pursue and whether to sell directly or through a local partner. The work can compare the options, shortlist potential partners and set out what to validate before committing resources.
