Advisory practices/Trading & commercial

Energy Trading & Commercial Advisory

Commercial decisions in physical energy depend on what can be delivered, at what cost and on which terms. We help trading, procurement and distribution teams compare options, understand their exposure and put agreed decisions into practice.

Enquire about this practice →

When to engage us

Energy traders, importers, distributors, industrial buyers and producers

  • You are reviewing a supply or offtake agreement and need a consistent view of cost, delivery obligations and commercial exposure.
  • A new trade route or sourcing option looks attractive on price, and you need to understand the impact of freight, storage, credit and operational constraints.
  • Your trading operation is growing and the handoffs between commercial, operations, finance and risk teams need clearer ownership.

What we do

Supply and offtake strategy

Assess sourcing options, buyer or supplier requirements, volume commitments, pricing structures and the terms that matter most in negotiation.

Landed cost and margin analysis

Compare product price, freight, storage, handling, financing and other route costs. Test the impact of changes in key assumptions.

Commercial terms and counterparty risk

Review payment and credit terms, delivery obligations and counterparty approval processes. Identify issues for legal and risk teams to resolve.

Trading workflows and implementation

Review how deals are approved, handed over and monitored. Clarify ownership, escalation and reporting across the trade lifecycle.

What you receive

We agree the outputs before work starts. Depending on the scope, they can include:

  • A landed cost and margin model with sensitivities
  • A comparison of supply or offtake options
  • A negotiation brief and commercial risk register
  • An implementation plan for trading workflows and controls

Illustrative engagement

Comparing two supply routes

A fuel importer considering two supply routes wants to understand how freight, discharge delays and payment terms affect margin and cash needs. An engagement can compare both routes on the same basis and identify the contract terms to address before committing.
